<h3>Midterm item 5: your repertoire. Choose one option, then submit a video or a Clef Runner code.</h3>
<p>The midterm repertoire comes from the Baroque period. Choose one of three options:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Option 1 (easier):</strong> BOTH Bagpipe (Anonymous, 17th century) AND Echo Dance (Philipp Hainhofer, 17th century).</li>
<li><strong>Option 2 (slightly more challenging):</strong> ONE of Old German Dance (Michael Praetorius, 1571-1621) OR Canario (Joachim von der Hofe, 17th century). Just one.</li>
<li><strong>Option 3 (harder):</strong> a harder Baroque keyboard piece, with my approval before you record it. Option 3 is a video only.</li>
</ul>
<p>The scores for Options 1 and 2 are in the Week 7 module. Play the whole piece, both hands, in the printed key, and <strong>play the repeats</strong>: Bagpipe and Canario repeat their sections, and a performance plays them. You are graded on accuracy and on keeping a steady pulse.</p>
<p>My play-along videos show the score, a keyboard and a visualizer for each piece: <a href="https://youtu.be/PQktUQB-pIY">Bagpipe</a>, <a href="https://youtu.be/iaOWqhNyoQM">Echo Dance</a>, <a href="https://youtu.be/506ak3dTjB8">Old German Dance</a>, <a href="https://youtu.be/ewrWBdxsOY8">Canario</a>.</p>

<h4>Way 1: Record a video (any option)</h4>
<p>Record yourself playing your piece or pieces on a piano or keyboard. Keep your hands and the keyboard in the frame. One take per piece is fine. For Option 1, record both pieces: one video with both, or two videos.</p>
<p>Choose a tempo you can keep steady from the first note to the last. A metronome is strongly encouraged, though not required.</p>
<p>To submit: upload the video file (or both files) under <strong>File Upload</strong>, or paste a link to an unlisted YouTube video under <strong>Text Entry</strong>. No cloud drive links. For Option 3, say the title and composer at the start.</p>

<h4>Way 2: Play it in Clef Runner (Options 1 and 2 only)</h4>
<p>Clef Runner is the sight-reading trainer: <a href="https://clef-runner.netlify.app">https://clef-runner.netlify.app</a></p>
<p><strong>You need a MIDI keyboard and a computer.</strong> Open the link in Chrome, Edge or Firefox with your keyboard plugged in. Safari and iPads will not work: neither can talk to a MIDI keyboard. Every station in the group piano lab (Room 269) has one.</p>
<ol>
<li>Choose your player profile, or make one. Your progress lives in that profile, in that browser, on that computer, so use the same one each time.</li>
<li>From the main menu press <strong>Homework</strong>.</li>
<li>Open <strong>Midterm Exam Repertoire</strong>, the first card on the list.</li>
<li>Pick a tempo (<strong>60, 80 or 100</strong>) and press <strong>Play</strong> beside a piece. Playing from that screen always uses both hands on the grand staff in the printed key, and the app plays the repeats for you, so a run is the whole piece.</li>
<li>Practice as much as you like. Only your best run on each piece counts. To practice slower than 60, play the piece in Repertoire at 40; those runs are practice and do not count.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>How the app grades it.</strong> A run counts at tempo 60 or faster. Option 1 is the average of your best Bagpipe and your best Echo Dance, so a piece you have not played counts 0 (one 90% piece alone is 45). Option 2 is your better run on Old German Dance or Canario. Your grade is the higher of the two options, so trying both never hurts. There is no minimum: a grade of 85 is 17 out of 20. The screen shows each option's score and your grade as you go.</p>
<p>To submit: on the same screen, scroll down, type your first name, last name and school ID, press <strong>Get Code</strong>, then <strong>Copy Code</strong>, and paste the code under <strong>Text Entry</strong>.</p>
<ul>
<li>Use the school ID your gradebook knows you by.</li>
<li>Paste ONLY the code, and ALL of it, from KTS1 to the very last letter.</li>
<li>The line under the code tells you what it is worth. You may submit again later with a better code.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Submit one way. Your last submission is the one graded.</h4>
<p>A video and a code are worth the same 20 points. If you submit more than once, only the last submission counts, so do not paste a code after uploading a video you want graded.</p>

<h4>Due Wednesday, October 14, 2026, 11:59 PM Eastern. You may turn it in early.</h4>

W. WHAT CLEF RUNNER CERTIFIES AND CANNOT
  It hears pitch and timing on the grand staff at the chosen tempo
  (60+), the whole piece with the repeats played (MIDREP-CR wrote them
  out: Bagpipe 16 bars, Canario 24 in the app; the page shows 8 / 16).
  It cannot see fingering, posture or dynamics, and "accuracy" is the
  app's notes-right percentage, not a steady-pulse judgment: the app's
  tempo is set for the student. So the two paths are not identical: the
  video grades pulse by ear, the app grades accuracy at a fixed tempo.
  The brief accepted that ("graded on OVERALL ACCURACY").

  CRITERION 1  ACCURACY (notes, rhythms, both hands, repeats played)   12
     12  Excellent   right notes and rhythms throughout; at most a slip
                     or two, recovered without stopping
      9  Good        a few wrong notes or rhythms; the piece is clearly
                     intact
      6  Fair        frequent errors, or a section missing (a repeat
                     skipped, one hand dropped for a passage)
      3  Weak        many errors; much of the piece not secure
      0  Missing     not played (for Option 1, a piece missing scores
                     about half of this criterion)

  CRITERION 2  STEADY PULSE (one tempo, start to finish)                 8
      8  Excellent   a steady pulse throughout, no stops or restarts
      6  Good        small hesitations, the pulse returns at once
      4  Fair        noticeable slowdowns at hard spots, or a restart
      2  Weak        the pulse comes and goes; frequent stops
      0  Missing     no performance to judge
