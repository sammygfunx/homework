===HTML===
<h3>HW #14: the twelve major scales, with their fingering. Do it in Scale Fingerprint.</h3>
<p>This replaces the old written scale quiz. For each of the twelve major scales, left hand first and then right hand, two octaves going up, you show <strong>the image of the scale</strong> (tap its keys on the keyboard; the black keys show as dots) and <strong>its fingering</strong> (the finger number for each of the 15 notes, lowest note first).</p>
<p>Open it here: <a href="https://scale-fingerprint.netlify.app/?quiz=1">https://scale-fingerprint.netlify.app/?quiz=1</a>. It works on a phone, a tablet or a computer. You do not need a MIDI keyboard. It is open book: the Scale Fingering Chart in the book is the answer.</p>
<p>Want to practice first? <a href="https://scale-fingerprint.netlify.app">https://scale-fingerprint.netlify.app</a> has games and a grid of all twelve keys for each hand. Practice never counts for a grade.</p>

<h4>How to do it</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the link. Type your first name, last name and <strong>Student ID</strong> (the one your gradebook knows you by).</li>
<li>The scales come in order: C, G, F, D, B-flat, A, E-flat, E, A-flat, B, D-flat, F-sharp/G-flat. For each one, do the left hand, then the right hand: tap the 15 keys, then fill the 15 finger boxes, lowest note first.</li>
<li>Finger boxes: one box is always selected. Tap a finger (1 to 5) and it fills that box and moves to the next, so just go in order. To fix one, tap that box and tap the right finger. The erase key (the backspace symbol) clears the selected box. A blank box counts as a wrong finger.</li>
<li>Use Prev and Next to move between scales. Your work is saved on your device, so a reload will not lose it.</li>
<li>Press <strong>Finish and Get Code</strong>. The results show every scale's answers. Then press <strong>Copy Code</strong> and paste ONLY the code, all of it, from KTS1 to the last letter, under <strong>Text Entry</strong> here.</li>
</ol>

<h4>How it is graded</h4>
<p>Every scale counts the same. In each scale, each hand is worth half. Within a hand, the notes are half and the fingering is half, and each of the 15 notes counts the same. A key you leave out or a key that is not in the scale is a note wrong; a finger box you leave blank is wrong. Partial credit adds up note by note, so a scale with one slip is nearly full marks. The fingering in the book's chart is the standard. Two places on the chart show a second correct choice in parentheses (B major left hand, the lowest B; F major right hand, the top F); either is right, and the app shows an alternative in parentheses so you know. A finger the chart does not show is wrong, even if you could play it.</p>

<h4>Do it as many times as you like. Your best code counts.</h4>
<p>You have unlimited attempts. A finished attempt cannot be reopened or edited; "Take it again" starts a blank one. The results screen shows your best code on that device; <strong>paste your best code</strong>. There is no video or paper way to do this homework, so use your own phone or tablet, or a computer in the lab.</p>

<h4>Due Friday, November 13, 2026, 11:59 PM Eastern. We do it together in class that day.</h4>
